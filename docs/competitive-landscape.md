# Competitive landscape & our wedge

*Quick-scan research on who's already near these ideas, and where the opening is.
Not exhaustive — enough to find the whitespace and sharpen the differentiation.*

---

## The market sorts into three clusters — and nobody sits in the middle

```
                 DEEP ECONOMY
                      |
        Offworld Trading Co.        <- deep, but PC/RTS, high floor,
        M.U.L.E. successors            single-screen, solo-ish, shows
        (Alpha Colony, Space Horse)    you the whole machine
                      |
   SOLO ----+---------+---------+---- SOCIAL / COUCH
            |                   |
   Food-truck / tycoon      Jackbox / AirConsole
   SIMS (single-player)     party games
   economy+theme, but       social + low floor, but
   no live rival, no table  no economy, no skill ceiling,
                      |        content decks run dry
                  SHALLOW
```

**The empty intersection** — *social + living-economy + low floor + real
ceiling, on a shared TV with private devices, at family length* — is essentially
unoccupied. That intersection is exactly what the four concepts target.

### Cluster 1 — Deep economy games (the M.U.L.E. bloodline)
- **Offworld Trading Company** (Mohawk Games, Soren Johnson) is *the* modern
  M.U.L.E. heir: a real-time market-manipulation RTS. Widely praised as deep, but
  reviewers note **13 resources, "lose your first 10 games," overwhelming**, and
  it's PC/solo-vs-AI or online — not a couch/family shared-screen game.
- **Alpha Colony**, **Space Horse**, and a mobile **M.U.L.E.** remake are direct
  tributes — small, turn-based, single-screen, hobbyist audiences.
- **Read:** they own *depth*, but they **show you the economy** (spreadsheets,
  micro, tutorials on "price fluctuation, patents, buying shares"). High floor,
  intimidating, not social-at-a-table.

### Cluster 2 — TV-hub + phone-controller party games
- **Jackbox Party Packs** and **AirConsole** (130+ games, phones as gamepads,
  room-code join) own the *format* and the *low floor*. Genres are trivia, word,
  drawing, arcade, party. **Faction Pirates** (papercraft, phone-controlled,
  trading/battling on the water) is the closest thing to an economy entry — and
  it's light.
- **Read:** they own *social + accessible*, but the games are **disposable** — no
  skill ceiling, you don't get *better*, and content decks (Jackbox prompts) run
  out. No living economy anywhere in the category.

### Cluster 3 — Solo tycoon / theme sims
- The food-truck theme is **crowded** here: *Food Truck Empire, Food Truck
  Simulator, Food Truck Monopoly, Food Truck Shop Simulator* — all
  **single-player tycoon/sims.** Economy + theme, but no live rival, no shared
  table, no drama.
- **Read:** they own *theme + economy*, but there's **no other human across the
  table** — the thing that makes M.U.L.E.'s market matter.

### Mario-Party-with-economy specifically
- *Pummel Party, Epic Party Quest* are minigame/chaos collections. The
  economy-management + party combo is explicitly noted as **rare/niche** — it
  basically doesn't exist.

## The wedge — how we're more entertaining than each cluster

We don't need a new mechanic nobody's thought of. The wedge is a **combination no
one is serving**, plus two things that are genuinely hard to copy.

**vs. Cluster 1 (Offworld / M.U.L.E. heirs):** they make you *stare at* the
economy. We **hide it inside a tactile verb** (the diegetic economy) and put the
drama on a **shared TV** with private iPad hands. Same depth, none of the
spreadsheet, plus a social table they don't have. *We take their ceiling and give
it a Jackbox floor.*

**vs. Cluster 2 (Jackbox / AirConsole):** they're social but disposable. We add a
**real skill ceiling** (the chess) and **replayability from a procedural living
economy** instead of a finite content deck — the market never repeats, so it
never runs dry. *We give a party night the legs of a strategy game.*

**vs. Cluster 3 (solo sims):** they're single-player grind. We add **a live human
rival across the table** and the **semi-coop tension** — the economy matters
because your kid is undercutting you, not because a script spawns customers.

### One-line positioning
> **The depth of Offworld, in the body of Jackbox, at the length of a family game
> night.** A living-economy strategy game that *feels* like a party game — 20
> minutes, never shows you a spreadsheet, chess underneath, never the same twice.

---

## Three moats (hardest-to-copy first)

1. **The diegetic-economy craft.** Hiding a real, endogenous market inside tactile
   verbs is *hard design work* — the sims show the numbers, the party games have
   no numbers. Getting a genuine market to *feel* like grabbing a spot and timing
   a beat is the thing a fast follower can't just clone from a screenshot.
2. **The format.** TV-hub + private iPad controllers is nearly unused for economy
   games. The **public/private information split** (the market is public on the
   TV; your hand, your bid, your plan are secret on your iPad) is a depth lever
   the PC economy games *can't* do and the party games *don't* exploit.
3. **Whitespace theme (esp. Overboard).** Fishing/overfishing-as-a-commons has
   *no* game competitor and its shared-collapse meter is the most diegetic of the
   four. Fresh on a store search *and* structurally elegant.

---

## What this means for the theme choice

- **Rush / food truck** is the strongest gut-fit, but it's the **most crowded
  theme on a storefront** — dozens of "Food Truck ___" sims. You'd be
  *structurally* different but *visually* lost in a sea of tycoon sims. Beatable
  with a strong art/tone identity, but it's a headwind.
- **Overboard / fishing** is the **best differentiation-per-effort**: uncontested
  theme, and the overfishing meter does the semi-coop marriage for free.
- **Boomtown / Encore** sit in between — fairly open, distinctive.

**Recommendation:** if standing out matters (it does, even for a family project
you might later share), **Overboard or Boomtown de-risk the "just another food-
truck game" problem** while keeping everything in the depth engine. Rush stays
viable if the food-truck *identity* is the emotional hook — just know you'd be
differentiating on feel, not concept.
