# Round 4 — Hiding the economy inside a tactile verb

*Your note: the economic model is right, but the missing ingredient is the
seamless marriage to gameplay. In M.U.L.E. you don't notice you're in an economy
— you're grabbing land and cursing that you released the mule a beat late. Two
principles fall out of that:*

- **Diegetic economy** — the economic choice is *disguised as a physical or
  timing action.* Players optimize a market by instinct and story, never by
  reading a price chart. The seven-lever engine from `depth-engine.md` runs
  underneath, invisible.
- **The timing leveler** — a commit-under-uncertainty moment (pick your spot; stop
  the meter on the right beat) that makes stories, occasionally humbles the
  expert, and gives the casual a real shot. This is M.U.L.E.'s land-grab and its
  "released the mule too late." It is *not optional flavor* — it's the thing that
  keeps the floor low while the ceiling stays high.

The engine (endogenous prices, a chokepoint, compounding, shapeable demand, a
semi-coop meter, lagged info) is fixed. What varies below is the **verb** — what
your hands and attention are actually doing — and the **timing moment.** Four
diverse worlds follow. All are TV-hub + iPad, character-driven, not cute.

---

## A. *"Boomtown"* — prospectors in a mining rush
**Gritty Western · the verb is STAKE & DIG**

**What you feel you're doing:** every round the assay bell rings and everyone
**stakes a claim** on the map at once, then works it with a **pan/dig** timing
minigame. You're a prospector chasing a strike — not a trader.

**Where the economy is hiding:** whatever the table digs up **floods the assay
office and craters its price.** You never see "supply elasticity" — you *feel* it
as "everybody struck gold, so gold's worth spit now… but I quietly staked the
coal seam nobody wanted, and the railroad just rolled in needing coal." (Levers
1, 4.)

**The timing leveler:** the **claim stampede** — simultaneous stake on a
countdown, adjacency matters, and the last-second grab over your kid is pure joy.
Then the **dig meter**: stop it on the sweet spot for a rich pull; overshoot and
the shaft **collapses** — the purest "I didn't time it right." (This is the
land-grab *and* the released-mule, both.)

**Chokepoint:** the mother lode vein everyone can see and nobody else can reach if
you've staked around it. **Semi-coop meter:** the town booms or busts — strip it
bare and the whole camp packs up, capping everyone.

**Casual vs. expert:** casual chases the shiny gold rush with everyone else;
expert corners the boring input the boom will *need next* and lets the crowd
crash their own strike.

## B. *"Overboard"* — a fishing fleet working the same waters
**Salty, all-ages · the verb is SPOT & CAST**

**What you feel you're doing:** pick a fishing spot on the TV map, then a
**cast-and-reel** timing minigame lands your haul. You're chasing the big one, not
running a business.

**Where the economy is hiding:** the catch floods the **dockside fish market** —
land a hold full of cod when everyone else did and cod is worthless; the lone
tuna sells for a fortune. Migration means spots shift, so last week's honey hole
is dead water now. You experience a live commodity market as *luck of the catch.*
(Levers 1, 6.)

**The timing leveler:** **spot-pick regret** (you commit blind to how rich a spot
is, and the reveal is the fun) plus the **cast/reel meter** — set the hook on the
right beat or the big one slips the line. Classic "argh, too early."

**Chokepoint:** the prized bluefin / the one reef that's always rich. **Semi-coop
meter — the standout here:** *overfishing empties the sea for everyone.* The
"don't sink the ship" meter is completely diegetic — it's literally the water
running dry. Tragedy-of-the-commons made fun, no explanation needed.

**Casual vs. expert:** casual fishes where the fish are today; expert reads the
migration a round ahead and is already sitting on tomorrow's spot — and knows
exactly how hard to fish before the waters crash.

## C. *"Encore"* — rival promoters booking a music festival
**Showbiz, witty, all-ages · the verb is GRAB A SLOT & WORK THE CROWD**

**What you feel you're doing:** snatch a **stage × time slot** on the festival
grid, book an act into it, then **hype the crowd** in a quick real-time push.
You're throwing the best party, not trading.

**Where the economy is hiding:** **crowd attention is the currency**, and it's
elastic — book a great act opposite another great act in the same slot and you
**split the crowd** (oversupply crashes both). The crowd's mood drifts (indie is
hot tonight, then it isn't), and you can **shape** it by hyping the genre you're
holding. (Levers 1, 4, 5.)

**The timing leveler:** the **slot scramble** — everyone grabs slots
simultaneously, and the headliner slot is a knife fight; misjudge and your
star plays to an empty field at 2pm. "You picked the wrong slot" is the whole
drama.

**Chokepoint:** the headline slot and the one star act everyone bids for.
**Semi-coop meter:** the festival's overall buzz — a flop night shrinks the crowd
for all the stages.

**Casual vs. expert:** casual books the act they like into an open slot; expert
counter-programs — books a cheap act into the slot the star is *not* in, and
scoops the whole undivided crowd nobody's serving.

## D. *"Rush"* — the Night Market food trucks, embodiment nailed
**Warm, relatable, all-ages · the verb is PARK & PLATE**

The food fusion from `depth-engine.md`, now built around the two principles.

**What you feel you're doing:** **drive and park** your truck on a corner, then a
real-time **plating rush** as the dinner crowd flows in — tap and drag to serve
the line before it wanders off. You're feeding a hungry crowd, not managing supply.

**Where the economy is hiding:** the **crowd's body language is the price chart.**
A long line at your window = high demand = good money. Three trucks on one corner
= a saturated block, and the crowd thins and drifts to the quiet street where
someone's underserved. You read demand off *where the people are standing.*
(Levers 1, 4.)

**The timing leveler:** the **park** — everyone commits their corner
simultaneously on a countdown; the great corner three people pick becomes a
bloodbath, and misjudging leaves you next to an empty lot. Plus the **surge**: the
crowd hits on a beat, and if your grill isn't hot (you didn't prep) you miss the
rush — "I wasn't ready in time."

**Chokepoint:** the prime corner and the game's rare **hero ingredient**.
**Semi-coop meter:** the market's foot traffic / vibrancy.

**Casual vs. expert:** casual parks where the crowd is; expert parks one block
over into unserved demand and lets rivals saturate the prime corner and crash
their own price.

## The pattern across all four

| Concept | The verb (what you feel) | The invisible economy | The timing leveler |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Boomtown** | Stake & dig | Extraction floods & craters prices | Claim stampede + dig-meter collapse |
| **Overboard** | Spot & cast | Catch floods the fish market | Blind spot-pick + hook timing |
| **Encore** | Grab a slot & hype | Crowd attention is elastic | Slot scramble for the headliner |
| **Rush** | Park & plate | Crowd position = the price chart | Simultaneous park + the surge |

Every one runs the same seven-lever engine underneath. None of them ever shows a
player a number they'd recognize as "the economy." Each has a commit-under-
pressure moment that levels the casual and makes the stories.
