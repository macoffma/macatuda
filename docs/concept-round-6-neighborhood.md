# Round 6 — "The Block": a neighborhood summer hustle

*Idea: kids' summer odd jobs — mow lawns, babysit, run a lemonade stand, rake
leaves. "Capitalistic Animal Crossing / Stardew lite." This may be the strongest
theme yet — here's why, how it maps onto our engine, and how it dodges the games
that already exist.*

Working title: **"The Block"** (alt: *Summer Hustle*, *Cul-de-Sac*, *Odd Jobs*).

---

## Why this one might be the best fit

It wins on the exact axes we've cared about most:

- **The most intuitive economy possible.** A lemonade stand is the *first economy
  every kid learns*. "It's hot → people are thirsty → sell lemonade" is a demand
  signal a six-year-old reads instantly. This is the ultimate "you don't notice
  you're in an economy" — no forecast, no translation.
- **Warm, all-ages, and not saccharine.** Nostalgic summer, real neighbors, rival
  kids — Animal Crossing warmth with *no talking animals* and no cuteness tax.
- **Character-driven for free.** The neighbors and the rival kids down the street
  are instant personalities.
- **No squeamishness.** No fish, nothing to catch, kill, or handle.

---

## It maps 1:1 onto the engine we already built and tuned

| Overboard piece | "The Block" |
|---|---|
| Go to a spot with hidden richness | **Pick a job** on the neighborhood map — you don't know how big the lawn / how bratty the kids / how thirsty the corner is until you take it (a rumor hints: "that's a big yard…"). |
| Press-your-luck gather + bust | **Work the job, push for the tip.** Each push earns more (mow another strip, serve another cup, entertain the kids longer) but risks a **flub** — hit the sprinkler, run out of lemons, the toddler melts down — that costs your **tip** and ends the job. "Go for the big tip, or wrap up clean." |
| Catch-of-the-Day demand | **The weather sets demand, diegetically.** Heat wave → lemonade pays double. Leaf-fall → raking pays double. Rainy day → indoor jobs (babysitting) pay more. Block party → everyone needs help. Kids *get* this instantly. |
| The renewable commons | **Neighborhood Goodwill** (see below). |
| Fun gear upgrades | The most legible progression yet: **better mower** (bigger lawns faster) · **bike/wagon** (reach more houses / first pick) · **flyers & a good sign** (see which houses have jobs) · **cooler/juicer** (fewer flubs). Money → better tools → earn more. |
| Coins / score | **Summer savings.** Most money at the end of the summer wins — if the block didn't sour. |
| AI rivals | The other neighborhood kids: the ruthless hustler, the sweet reliable one, the lazy-but-lucky goofball. |

The greybox reskins to this almost directly — job = spot, work-the-job = reel,
weather-demand = catch-of-the-day, tools = gear.

## The clever part: "Neighborhood Goodwill" is the commons

Fishing's elegance was the renewable commons (overfish and it collapses). The
neighborhood's version is **Goodwill** — the block's shared mood:

- **Do good work and be neighborly** → goodwill rises → more jobs appear, tips get
  bigger, for *everyone*.
- **Cut corners, flood the block (five lemonade stands), or squeeze the neighbors**
  → goodwill craters → neighbors stop hiring, tips dry up, the summer sours for
  *everyone*.

It's a real tragedy-of-the-commons on the block's goodwill — and it does something
lovely: **you have to hustle *and* be a good neighbor.** That naturally checks the
"too capitalistic" worry, adds the semi-coop "don't wreck the shared thing"
tension, and lands a nice family message without being preachy.

## Does it already exist? (the honest competitor read)

- **Lemonade tycoons are a crowded niche** — the classic *Lemonade Stand*,
  *Lemonade Tycoon*, and a new Steam *Lemonade Stand* (219 Studios) billed as a
  "multiplayer economy, deep strategy." But these are **solo/online single-screen
  tycoons about the one chore.**
- **"Odd Jobs: On The Lot"** is a **chaotic physics 4-player co-op** (push
  trolleys, blast grime) — a party *action* game, not a light-economy game.
- **Stardew / Animal Crossing** — single-player cozy sims, not short competitive
  couch sessions.

**The gap:** the *neighborhood-variety* hustle (many chores, not just lemonade) +
**TV-hub & iPad party** format + **M.U.L.E. easy-but-deep economy** + the
**goodwill commons** is unoccupied. Same three moats as our earlier landscape
scan: the public/private format, the diegetic-economy craft, and a synthesis
nobody's shipping.

### One caution
"Lemonade stand game" as a phrase is crowded, so we should **lead with "summer on
the block," not "lemonade,"** and keep the *chore variety* front and center — the
same way we'd avoid looking like "just another food-truck sim." Lemonade is one
job among many, not the whole game.

This is my pick of all the themes so far — it's the most intuitive economy, the
warmest, the most character-rich, squeamish-proof, and the "be a good neighbor"
