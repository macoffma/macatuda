# Concept Round 2 — Less cute, less M.U.L.E.-shaped

*Feedback on Round 1: the critters-in-a-valley theme is too twee, and a
"colonists produce and trade commodities" loop is exactly where the existing
M.U.L.E.-likes live. This round keeps the good bones — TV hub + iPad
controllers, 15–30 min, live tension, personality, AI chaos — but skews older
and, crucially, several of these **replace the core loop** so they don't read as
a reskin.*

We keep from Round 1: the platform pattern, the live-market/auction idea (where
it fits), semi-coop "don't sink the ship" tension, and events as a story engine.
We throw out: cuteness, and the assumption that the loop must be
produce-a-commodity-and-sell-it.

---

## How each one departs from M.U.L.E.

| Concept | Core loop | What replaces "produce & trade" | Tone / age |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spin** | Bid for stories, spin the narrative | Currency is *public attention*, not goods | Satirical · teens+ |
| **Runway** | Ride a hype bubble, cash out before the crash | The *market itself* is the antagonist | Comedic · teens+ |
| **Long Haul** | Route + press-your-luck smuggling | Spatial logistics + shared hazard | Adventurous · 10+ |
| **The Job** | Build a crew, beat the alarm, split the loot | Risk + cutthroat negotiation, no production at all | Tense/funny · 12+ |
| **Grease** | Two-sided market: buy supply, serve demand | Adds a *customer/demand* side M.U.L.E. never had | Warm/funny · all ages |

---

## A. *"Spin"* — rival newsrooms in a tabloid war
**Satirical · teens & up · verbal, quick-witted**

You each run a scrappy news outlet. The TV breaks **stories** each round (a
celebrity scandal, a city hall leak, a UFO over the mall). You secretly bid your
limited reporters/cash for the stories you want, then choose an **angle**:
straight, sensational, or hit-piece. Angles move a shared **National Mood** dial
and your own **Reputation** vs. **Clicks**.

- **The engine (not M.U.L.E.):** the currency is *public attention*. You're not
  making widgets — you're competing over a shared pool of stories and spinning a
  public-mood meter that everyone's scores ride on.
- **The tension:** cheap sensationalism wins Clicks now but tanks Reputation
  (and can crash the National Mood — the semi-coop "don't burn it all down").
- **TV:** front pages assemble live, the Mood meter swings, a scandal ticker runs.
- **iPad:** your newsroom — assign reporters, pick angles, plant a secret op-ed.
- **AI chaos:** a rival tabloid that will print *anything*; a stodgy paper of
  record that punishes your lies by fact-checking you.

## B. *"Runway"* — the startup hype bubble
**Comedy/satire · teens & up · adults will lose it**

You're founders in a frothy tech scene. Pitch absurd startups
("Uber, but for soup"), raise rounds, hire, and — the whole game — decide when to
**cash out before the bubble pops.** The market index inflates on hype and
periodically **crashes**, wiping out anyone still over-leveraged.

- **The engine (not M.U.L.E.):** it's a **boom/bust press-your-luck**, not a
  trade economy. The market is the antagonist. You want to be greedy… but not the
  one holding the bag when it corrects.
- **The tension:** burn rate is your consumption pressure — sit on cash and you
  fall behind; over-extend and a crash ruins you. A big enough crash tanks the
  *whole* table (semi-coop).
- **TV:** a live market index, a "trend of the round" ticker (AI! crypto! soup!),
  crash klaxons.
- **iPad:** your cap table, secret raise/cash-out moves, your startup's dumb name.
- **AI chaos:** a hype-man VC who pumps everything; a doomer who shorts the market
  and profits from crashes.

## C. *"Long Haul"* — smugglers running the black market
**Adventurous, a little edgy · 10+ · not cutesy**

You captain a hauler moving contraband between ports whose prices swing wildly.
Buy low at one port, race to sell high at another — while a **Patrol** roams the
map (shared hazard, visible to all on the TV). Get caught with a full hold and
you lose the cargo.

- **The engine:** **route optimization + press-your-luck**, with the market as
  the payoff rather than the whole game. It's the "spatial planning brain" your
  older kids might like, kept short.
- **The tension:** the fuller your hold and the higher the price, the more you
  *don't* want to run into the Patrol. Events reroute it or spike a port's demand.
- **TV:** the sea/space map, ship positions, the Patrol, the price board.
- **iPad:** your hold, your planned route, a one-time "bribe/bluff the patrol" card.
- **AI chaos:** a reckless runner who floods a port and craters its price; a
  cautious trader who always seems to be at the right port before you.

## D. *"The Job"* — one heist, one night
**Tense and funny · 12+ (the most grown-up of the set)**

The purest departure. No production, no commodities. You assemble a **crew**,
case a job, and pull it off in real time as a shared **Alarm/Heat** meter climbs.
Push for more loot and the heat rises; bail early and you leave money on the
table. At the end you **split the take in a live, cutthroat negotiation** — and
someone might **double-cross**.

- **The engine (not M.U.L.E. at all):** **risk + negotiation + betrayal.** The
  drama is the rising alarm and the argument over the split, not an economy.
- **The tension:** blow the job (heat maxes) and *everyone* walks away with
  nothing — but the split is zero-sum, so cooperation is fragile by design.
- **TV:** the vault, the guards, the Heat meter, the loot pile.
- **iPad:** your secret specialist role, your gear, and your private "how much I'm
  demanding / who I'm betraying" choices.
- **AI chaos:** a loose-cannon crewmate who trips alarms; a cool pro who always
  demands the biggest cut.
- **Note:** betrayal games are the spiciest socially — great with the right table,
  worth gauging whether it's fun-tense or actually-upsetting for your crew. Easy
  to ship a "no-betrayal, just-the-heist" variant.

## E. *"Grease"* — rival late-night food joints
**Warm and funny · genuinely all-ages · relatable, not childish**

You run competing food trucks/diners on one street. Unlike M.U.L.E., there are
**two markets**: you **buy ingredients** (prices spike with events) *and* **serve
a shifting crowd** of customers with their own cravings. Read the crowd, stock
the right stuff, out-serve your rivals during the rush.

- **The engine:** adds the **demand/service side** M.U.L.E. never had — it's as
  much about anticipating customers as trading goods. Feels fresh even though
  the "market" DNA is visible.
- **The tension:** a bad ingredient buy or a misread crowd leaves you slammed and
  empty-handed during the rush; the health inspector / a viral post shakes
  everyone up (events).
- **TV:** the street, the crowd flowing in, the ingredient price board, the rush timer.
- **iPad:** your kitchen — menu, prep, and pricing.
- **AI chaos:** a price-war rival who undercuts everyone; a gimmick truck that
  chases every trend and yanks the crowd around.

## F. Bonus seed — *"Blockbusters"* — city-block developers
**Relatable · 10+ · Monopoly-adjacent but short and less brutal**

Buy lots on a shared block and build businesses; your buildings **raise or lower
your neighbors' property values** (ripple interdependence — the M.U.L.E. "you
need each other" idea, made spatial and a bit passive-aggressive). A new subway
stop or a flood (events) reshapes the whole block. Quick property market to buy
and flip.

## Reactions I'd want from you

- **Tone check:** *Spin*/*Runway* are the wittiest and oldest-skewing; *Grease*
  is the warmest all-ages pick; *The Job* is the spiciest; *Long Haul* is the
  adventure/logistics one. Which *vibe* fits family game night?
- **Which core loop sounds most fun** — attention/spin, bubble/press-luck,
  route/smuggle, heist/negotiate, or two-sided market? That matters more than the
  theme; we can re-skin any of them.
- Any of these can still keep the **live-auction moment** as one phase if we love
  it — it's not all-or-nothing.
