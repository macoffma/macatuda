# Bringing M.U.L.E.'s hands-on joy to The Block

*The mechanics are good but the game is clicky. M.U.L.E.'s magic wasn't the
market — it was the **ritual and the journey**: walk to the depot, grab your
mule, outfit it, walk it to your plot (and pray it doesn't bolt), slam it down.
Physical cause-and-effect, light dexterity, slapstick, a felt trip. This is how
we get that back **without changing the product.***

---

## The principle

Keep everything — neighborhood hustle, TV + phone, knock/stand/tips, the daylight
budget, the garage sale. Change only the **execution layer**: replace
*instant menu-teleport-and-resolve* with **short embodied rituals** — a journey, a
prep, a doing, a chance for slapstick. Every beat stays a few seconds so the
strategy never bogs down. The decisions are still the game; the *doing* becomes
something your hands enjoy.

---

## M.U.L.E.'s beats → The Block's beats

| M.U.L.E. moment | The Block moment |
|---|---|
| Walk your colonist across the map | **Pedal your bike** across the block toward the job — you don't teleport, you *go* |
| The real-time land-grab (run to claim) | **The door-race** — pump to beat a rival to the good house; the loser peels off |
| Trek to the depot, grab a mule | **Load up** — grab the mower, fill the pitcher, hitch the wagon |
| Outfit the mule for a resource | **Kit out** with the tools you won at the garage sale |
| Walk the mule to the plot — *it can escape!* | **Haul the wagon** to the corner — keep it steady or the lemonade spills (the slapstick) |
| Install the mule, watch it work | **Set up & do it** — plant your stand, or start mowing (the micro-game we already have) |

Same journey shape, kid-summer skin.

---

## The tactile beats, moment by moment

- **Travel = pedal.** Tap/hold to ride toward your destination; the trip is short
  but *felt*. Room for a shortcut, a hill, a puddle to coast over.
- **Door-race = a real dash.** When two kids aim at the same door, it's a sprint,
  not an auto "closest wins." You *earn* the good job — or watch it get sniped and
  know exactly why. (This is the plot-grab, finally embodied.)
- **Prep/outfit = a load-up ritual.** Grab the rake off the garage wall, fill the
  pitcher, hitch the wagon. A couple of satisfying drags. Ties straight to the
  tools you won at the garage sale.
- **Haul = the mule-escapes beat.** Drag your wagon of lemonade across the block;
  a little balance/spill risk if you barrel over a bump. Walking a dog? It tugs;
  you steer. Comedy + light skill.
- **Doing = the job's micro-game.** Mowing's timing bar (built). Rake = swipe the
  leaves into a pile. Lemonade = pour and serve the rush. Babysit = whack-a-tantrum.
- **Set up / tear down = physical placement.** Arrange your stand, plant your sign
  — the little "slam the mule down" payoff.

---

## Who does what (the TV/phone division of the *fun*)

- **Phone = the hands.** Pedalling, steering, hauling, pouring, the micro-games.
  Your dexterity, private and tactile.
- **TV = the spectacle.** All the kids zooming around the block at once, colliding
  at doors, a wagon spilling, a stand popping up on the corner. The family watches
  the neighborhood come *alive*. **This is the "something fun on the TV that pulls
  it all together"** — the movement is the show.

The button-pushing dissolves into a little bustling town everyone can watch.

---

## Guardrails (so it stays fun, not fiddly)

- **Every beat ≤ a few seconds**, forgiving; a kid is never hard-punished.
- **Dexterity affects the tip/margin, not the base** — you always get paid for the
  job (same rule as the mowing micro-game). Skill is upside, not a gate.
- **Vary the beats** so they don't get repetitive; not every job needs every ritual.
- **An "assist/auto" option** for the youngest or a fast game — hold to auto-pedal,
  auto-steady the wagon.
- **The strategy layer is untouched** — which job, reading cues, the daylight
  budget, the auction. These are execution flourishes, not new systems.

---

## What I built to feel it

First try was a 1-D pedal race ("Race to the Door") — but a button you time isn't
exciting, and it wasn't the point. The real idea is **hauling your loaded cart
through the streets to the house before the others get there.**

So: **"Haul to the Door"** — a top-down, **drag-to-steer** race. You pull a cart
that has *weight and swing*: whip a corner and it fishtails and slows you, so the
skill is taking smooth, wide lines, staying on the road (grass is slow), and
dodging the 🐕 — all while a rival's cart converges on the same lawn. That's the
M.U.L.E. "walk your mule to the plot" feeling, and it works fine on a phone: the
whole thing is one thumb dragging. If *that* feels good, it's the template for
turning every "tap a house" into a little haul — and on the TV, four carts
weaving toward four doors at once is the show.
